# HLD — rhydra tranche 6b: HEVC (single stream)

Decision record: `wrk_docs/2026.08.20 - DEC - rhydra codec choice - HEVC 4-2-0, two
tiles at 5K.md` (measurements). Gating question closed by
`wrk_docs/2026.08.20 - SPIKE - does HEVC 4-2-0 damage text at 5K.md`.
Parent: `wrk_docs/2026.08.18 - HLD - native product shape and session ownership.md`.

## 1. What this delivers

**Revision 2, after two adversarial reviews. Revision 1 proposed two-tile video
and was wrong at the premises; see §1b.** rhydra encodes **HEVC Main, 4:2:0,
8-bit, single stream**. Dirty rects are untouched.

Measured basis: HEVC beats H.264 at every size from 1080p up — 2.2 ms vs 3.6 ms
p50 at 1920×1080, the mode the product actually runs — and H.264 cannot encode 5K
on this hardware at all, which matters the day the display grows.

## 1b. Why two-tile video is DEFERRED, not built

Revision 1 designed a tile protocol, a composition rule, a dual-decoder
lifecycle and a synchronised IDR policy. Reviews killed it on facts that were in
the code the whole time and that revision 1 never checked.

**It could never have fired.** `agent.rs:37` pins `DESIRED_MODE` at
**1920×1080** — "the wire's proven frame geometry" — and the reconciler
re-applies it every tick. The IddCx driver offers 1920×1080 and 2560×1440 and
nothing else. The client discards resize requests (`session.rs:497-503`, "the
host owns its resolution"). Revision 1's tile threshold was `width > 2560`.
**Under the shipping configuration `MSG_VIDEO_TILE` is unreachable.** Getting to
5K is unscoped work in three other places, none of which revision 1 mentioned.

**And it would have shipped a half-stale screen.** Three findings, each verified
against the code:

1. Two tiles share one `capture_seq`, and the client's exactness gate is
   `s > e` (`session.rs:614-619`). **The second tile is suppressed by design** —
   half the screen never updates, and the only counter that moves is
   `suppressed`, which is normal and expected.
2. "Decode into the surface as tiles arrive, present when complete" is
   impossible here: `session.rs:13-19` states that **surface 0 IS the canvas**.
   There is no back buffer, and the AU path exists specifically to avoid an 8 MB
   copy. Revision 1's two sentences contradicted each other, and its "tiling is
   free on the client" silently omitted a per-frame full-surface copy the
   single-stream path does not pay.
3. `send.rs:242-249` hoists every `Rects` message ahead of frames in a batch, so
   a rect update can land *between* two tiles of one frame. On a busy desktop
   that yields a permanently stale half-screen with no error.

**Deferring removes all three.** One AU per `capture_seq` is exactly the model
the client already implements and tests. That is the strongest argument for the
simplification: it is not merely less work, it is less wrongness.

**What would justify revisiting**, in order: the display actually growing past
2560 wide; and one cheap counter this design adds — *what fraction of frames are
full-frame encodes, and at what width*. Nobody has that number today, and it is
the number that decides tiling.

## 1c. 4:2:0 — deferred, in Arthur's words, not rejected in mine

Revision 1 wrote "**4:4:4 is rejected, not deferred**". The decision record
Arthur agreed says the opposite: "**4:4:4 is deferred**, not rejected… and is
reversible." Arthur agreed *"We go HEVC"*; he did not agree to close D3D12 Video
Encode. **Revision 1 amended a signed decision record unilaterally and this
revision reverts it.**

The spike's own recommendation is narrower than revision 1 made it: *"Do not
build D3D12 Video Encode **for text quality**. If it is built later, let the
reason be AV1, vendor-neutrality, or 10-bit — not this."* One rationale is
closed. The subsystem is not.

**The headline evidence is also weaker than revision 1 claimed**, and this is
worth recording because it was quoted as settled fact:

- The "real Windows 5K capture" (`baseline/freerdp-reference/ref2-full.png`) is
  a **macOS screenshot of a FreeRDP window**, so its chroma had already been
  through a lossy codec, a client renderer and the compositor before anything
  was measured. Measuring chroma loss on chroma already destroyed upstream is
  circular for this question.
- It is tagged **DCI-P3**, while the method computed ΔE2000 "in sRGB". That
  biases ΔE *low* — in the direction that produced the headline.
- ΔE p95 0.48 sitting *below* the 0.52 rounding floor is an artefact of smooth
  content: subsampling cannot subtract error, but it can low-pass rounding noise
  out of a gradient. "Indistinguishable on a gradient" is the honest reading.
- "4:4:4 measured fractionally worse" rests on 0.68 vs 0.70 — one frame, one
  encoder, one image. That is noise cited as evidence, in a repo whose rules
  require n, min, median and max for any number reasoned from later.

**The named re-test**, which is what "deferred" obliges us to state: Intel's
hardware MFT at low-latency CBR against a real desk capture, over a long P-frame
run rather than one intra frame. The spike says explicitly that rate control
"does not transfer" and lists "anything temporal" as untested. Each is an
afternoon's work and none is an acceptance criterion yet.

## 2. The wire: one line changes

No new message type. HEVC access units ride `MSG_VIDEO_SEQ` exactly as H.264
ones do — same framing, same `u64` capture sequence, same one-AU-per-message
rule. Only the bitstream inside changes.

**`WIRE_VERSION` goes 3 → 4.** That single line is the whole compatibility
story, and revision 1 omitted it while writing three paragraphs about the
hazard it prevents.

## 3. Compatibility — the mechanism already exists

Revision 1 proposed announcing the codec in the stats header and failing the
connect on a codec the client cannot decode. **The header has no codec field,
and adding one would be strictly worse than what already ships.**

`probe.rs:32-33` sets `WIRE_VERSION_MIN = WIRE_VERSION_MAX = 3`, gated at
`probe.rs:205` with a direction-aware `VersionMismatch`. Bump the constant on
both ends and **both** directions fail loudly at connect, before a single access
unit, with no new field and no new code.

Why a `codec` field would be worse: `ServerHeader` documents that "unknown
fields are ignored by serde's default", and it carries `#[serde(default)]`. So
whichever default is chosen is wrong in one direction —

- default `h264`: a new host's HEVC reaches a client that thinks it is H.264.
- default `hevc`: **a new client against an old host** builds an HEVC decoder,
  receives H.264 AUs, never finds VPS/SPS/PPS under HEVC NAL numbering, fails
  every AU, connects successfully and **paints black with no error**.

That second case is the reverse direction revision 1 never considered, and it is
verbatim the failure its own §3 existed to prevent. A codec name in the header
is fine as *diagnostics*. It must never be the gate.

## 4. What the client shows

`session.rs:52` hardcodes `CODEC_LABEL = "AVC (rhydra)"` — the label the title
bar and HUD display. Ship without changing it and **the HUD lies about the
codec**, in a tranche whose §6 argues that a codec lying about itself is worse
than one that refuses. The label becomes a property of the negotiated stream,
not a constant.

The loop's settled answer that "transport is always visible (title/HUD/
stage-json)" points the same way.

## 5. Encoding stays exactly as it is

Revision 1 contained a table of when to tile. There is no tiling, and there is
also **no full-repaint classifier to hang one on**: `pipeline.rs:693-694` states
it plainly — *"The fast path is an overlay, not a branch: whatever happens here,
the frame still goes on to convert, encode and send as H.264 below."*

Every captured frame already produces a full-frame access unit; dirty rects are
an **additional** message carrying raw BGRA, capped at 32 rects / 96 KB. There
is no dirty-rect *encode* path anywhere in the server.

**This matters beyond the deleted table.** The decision record's "dirty rects
from DXGI drive small encodes at fractions of a millisecond", the paced spike's
"regions, not frames", and revision 1's "dirty rects remain the main path" all
describe an architecture that does not exist. The paced spike's 256×256, 640×480
and 720p cells measure a path nothing calls. Recorded here rather than left for
the next reader to rediscover; whether to *build* region encoding is a separate
question this tranche does not answer.

## 6. Host encoder

The `Encoder` trait, the async `IMFMediaEventGenerator` contract, the
low-latency/CBR/B=0 settings and the D3D11 texture input all carry over.

**What actually changes, beyond the subtype:**

- `encode.rs:667-675` hardcodes `MFVideoFormat_H264` in the `MFTEnumEx` output
  type info; `encode.rs:819` sets the subtype; `encode.rs:827` sets
  `MF_MT_MPEG2_PROFILE` to an **H.264 enum value** that is not the HEVC one.
- `MF_MT_MPEG2_LEVEL` is **not set at all** today. That has never mattered
  because nothing has run above 1080p. It stays not-mattering at 1920×1080, and
  becomes load-bearing the day the display grows — noted so it is not a surprise
  then.

**`annexb.rs` is the real hazard, and revision 1 never named the file.** Every
function there is H.264-shaped, and under HEVC **none of them fails — they
succeed at doing nothing:**

```
nal_type = b & 0x1F          // H.264: type is the low 5 bits of ONE byte
                             // HEVC:  header is TWO bytes, type = (b[0] >> 1) & 0x3F
```

So HEVC's VPS (32) reads as 0, SPS (33) as 2, PPS (34) as 4 — never 5, 7 or 8.
Therefore `has_parameter_sets` returns false for every HEVC AU, the in-band
cache never populates, `from_sequence_header` returns None so the out-of-band
fallback is gone, and `param_sets_prepended` reads false forever — **which is
indistinguishable from perfect health.**

The entire parameter-set safety net compiles, runs, reports green and does
nothing. It would surface only when the encoder emits a bare IDR — most likely
at a mid-session `MF_E_TRANSFORM_STREAM_CHANGE` renegotiation, which is exactly
when the fallback was supposed to save us. `ParameterSets` is a two-field struct
and HEVC needs three.

**Also unverified and worth checking on the box before coding:** Media
Foundation may publish the HEVC sequence header hvcC-style rather than
start-code-delimited. `from_sequence_header` returns None either way today.

**Never trust `SetOutputType`.** Intel's MFTs accept `Main_444_8` and silently
emit `chroma_format_idc=1`. Negotiation must parse the first keyframe's SPS and
assert what it asked for — and note HEVC's profile lives in
`profile_tier_level`, not H.264's byte-2 `profile_idc`, so `mf-caps-probe`'s
parsers need the same generalisation for the same reason.

## 7. Client decoder — more than a subtype swap

The decoder is a `VTDecompressionSession` typed as
`ironrdp_egfx::decode::H264Decoder` (`src/native/session.rs:29,211`) — an
RDP-specific trait with an H.264-shaped contract, borrowed by the native path.

1. `kCMVideoCodecType_HEVC`, and an **hvcC** format description from
   VPS/SPS/PPS via the HEVC variant taking **three** parameter sets, not the
   H.264 call taking two (`h264.rs:513-520`).
2. **The same NAL-header arithmetic bug exists client-side** (`h264.rs:142-144`,
   `& 0x1F`; `parameter_sets` looks for types 7/8). Revision 1 got the hvcC part
   right and missed this — which is where all the *silent* failures live.
3. rhydra's own `VideoDecoder` trait, so the native path stops depending on an
   RDP crate's H.264 abstraction.
4. **Not free:** `h264.rs:692-701` runs `yuv420_to_rgba` on the CPU, on the net
   thread, per frame. The decode bench measured VideoToolbox only, so no quoted
   number includes it.

**Ordering constraint:** this rewires `src/native/session.rs`, which
`task/20260820-TSK-HUM-fix-native-session-stats` also touches. That lands first.

## 8. Acceptance criteria

Against the repo's rules: a real server for protocol work, a number *and its
conditions*, a distribution rather than one run, and soak-class evidence for a
soak-class claim.

- **AC1 — the bitstream is what we asked for.** The first keyframe's SPS parses
  as Main, 4:2:0, 8-bit, read from `profile_tier_level`. Asserted **in the
  product**, not only in a probe, because Intel accepts profiles it does not
  honour.
- **AC2 — the parameter-set net actually works under HEVC.** `nal_type` returns
  32/33/34 for VPS/SPS/PPS; `has_parameter_sets` is true for a real keyframe;
  `param_sets_prepended` moves when the sets are absent in-band. *This exists
  because the failure mode is reporting green while doing nothing, and no
  end-to-end test would catch it.*
- **AC3 — a real desktop renders correctly on quench**, release build, over the
  real MFT: a session connects, a text-heavy window renders, and a capture
  matches an sRGB reference within a stated ΔE tolerance. Names the metric and
  the tolerance, which revision 1 did not.
- **AC4 — both compatibility directions fail loudly.** Old client + new host,
  **and new client + old host**, each refused at connect naming the versions.
  Revision 1 tested only the direction that already worked.
- **AC5 — latency in the real pipeline**, not the bench: frame-complete p50 and
  p95 with n ≥ 30, min/median/max, release build, quench, stated content and
  bitrate. HEVC against the H.264 baseline at 1920×1080.
- **AC6 — bitrate, with its conditions.** Bytes/second at a stated
  `bitrate_kbps`, over a stated content type, as a distribution. This is the
  number that decides whether tranche 6's priority claim about the shared ssh
  tunnel is a real problem — and it is currently unmeasured.
- **AC7 — the counter that decides tiling.** What fraction of frames are
  full-frame encodes, and at what width. Nobody has this number; it is what
  makes the §1b deferral revisitable on evidence rather than on a hunch.
- **AC8 — an hour under load, not a unit test.** Product requirement 3 is
  latency degrading over a session, and the untested chroma case is precisely a
  long P-frame run. Decode errors, `param_sets_prepended`, and frame-complete
  p95 sampled across the run.

## 9. What this does not do

- **No two-tile video.** Deferred with its evidence and a revisit condition
  (§1b).
- **No 4:4:4 decision.** Deferred in Arthur's words with a named re-test (§1c).
- **No AV1.** No encoder MFT is registered on quench; VideoToolbox has no
  4:4:4/4:2:2 AV1 decoder.
- **No H.264 as a second rhydra codec.** Note the saving is one-sided: the
  client keeps a full H.264 VideoToolbox decoder regardless, because the RDP
  path stays on AVC444.
- **No region encoding.** §5 records that it does not exist despite three
  documents describing it; building it is a separate question.
- **mdrdp's RDP path is untouched.**

## 10. Review record

**Revision 1: rejected by both reviewers.** Every finding below was verified
against the code before being accepted.

**Reviewer 1 (protocol and concurrency)** — the tile design collides with the
client's exactness gate so the second tile is suppressed by design; "present
when every tile has landed" is impossible because surface 0 is the canvas;
`send.rs` hoists rects ahead of frames so a rect can land between two tiles of
one frame; there is no way to distinguish an incomplete tile set from a late
one; `annexb.rs` is H.264-shaped throughout and fails **green** under HEVC; the
compat gate should be `WIRE_VERSION`, not a defaulted header field; and AC2/AC3/
AC4 were theatre.

**Reviewer 2 (scope and evidence)** — the tile path is unreachable because the
agent pins 1920×1080; "dirty rects are the main path" is contradicted by the
pipeline's own comment; the 4:4:4 rejection overstates a spike whose one real
capture is a P3 screenshot of an RDP client window; the headline p50 excludes
the tile-split copy and omits the tail; the HUD would lie about the codec; and
revision 1 amended a decision record Arthur had signed.

**What changed:** two-tile video deferred (§1b), which deletes three of
reviewer 1's critical findings outright; 4:4:4 restored to Arthur's "deferred"
with a named re-test (§1c); `WIRE_VERSION` bump replacing the codec-field design
(§3); `annexb.rs` named as the real hazard (§6); the client-side NAL arithmetic
added (§7); and the acceptance criteria rewritten so they can fail.

**Pending:** a cross-family Codex review. On tranche 6 it found eleven things
two same-family reviewers had missed, clustered in Windows-domain specifics —
which is what §6 and §7 are made of.

**Status: revision 2, not yet approved.**
