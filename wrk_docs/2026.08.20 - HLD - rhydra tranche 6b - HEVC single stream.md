# HLD — rhydra tranche 6b: HEVC (single stream)

Decision record: `wrk_docs/2026.08.20 - DEC - rhydra codec choice - HEVC 4-2-0, two
tiles at 5K.md` (measurements). Gating question closed by
`wrk_docs/2026.08.20 - SPIKE - does HEVC 4-2-0 damage text at 5K.md`.
Parent: `wrk_docs/2026.08.18 - HLD - native product shape and session ownership.md`.

## 1. What this delivers

**Revision 2, after two adversarial reviews. Revision 1 proposed two-tile video
and was wrong at the premises; see §1b.** rhydra encodes **HEVC Main, 4:2:0,
8-bit, single stream**. Dirty rects are untouched.

Measured basis: HEVC beats H.264 at every size from 1080p up — 2.2 ms vs 3.6 ms
p50 at 1920×1080, the mode the product actually runs — and H.264 cannot encode 5K
on this hardware at all, which matters the day the display grows.

## 1b. Why two-tile video is DEFERRED, not built

Revision 1 designed a tile protocol, a composition rule, a dual-decoder
lifecycle and a synchronised IDR policy. Reviews killed it on facts that were in
the code the whole time and that revision 1 never checked.

**It could never have fired.** `agent.rs:37` pins `DESIRED_MODE` at
**1920×1080** — "the wire's proven frame geometry" — and the reconciler
re-applies it every tick. The IddCx driver offers 1920×1080 and 2560×1440 and
nothing else. The client discards resize requests (`session.rs:497-503`, "the
host owns its resolution"). Revision 1's tile threshold was `width > 2560`.
**Under the shipping configuration `MSG_VIDEO_TILE` is unreachable.** Getting to
5K is unscoped work in three other places, none of which revision 1 mentioned.

**And it would have shipped a half-stale screen.** Three findings, each verified
against the code:

1. Two tiles share one `capture_seq`, and the client's exactness gate is
   `s > e` (`session.rs:614-619`). **The second tile is suppressed by design** —
   half the screen never updates, and the only counter that moves is
   `suppressed`, which is normal and expected.
2. "Decode into the surface as tiles arrive, present when complete" is
   impossible here: `session.rs:13-19` states that **surface 0 IS the canvas**.
   There is no back buffer, and the AU path exists specifically to avoid an 8 MB
   copy. Revision 1's two sentences contradicted each other, and its "tiling is
   free on the client" silently omitted a per-frame full-surface copy the
   single-stream path does not pay.
3. `send.rs:242-249` hoists every `Rects` message ahead of frames in a batch, so
   a rect update can land *between* two tiles of one frame. On a busy desktop
   that yields a permanently stale half-screen with no error.

**Deferring removes all three.** One AU per `capture_seq` is exactly the model
the client already implements and tests. That is the strongest argument for the
simplification: it is not merely less work, it is less wrongness.

**What would justify revisiting**, in order: the display actually growing past
2560 wide; and one cheap counter this design adds — *what fraction of frames are
full-frame encodes, and at what width*. Nobody has that number today, and it is
the number that decides tiling.

## 1c. 4:2:0 — deferred, in Arthur's words, not rejected in mine

Revision 1 wrote "**4:4:4 is rejected, not deferred**". The decision record
Arthur agreed says the opposite: "**4:4:4 is deferred**, not rejected… and is
reversible." Arthur agreed *"We go HEVC"*; he did not agree to close D3D12 Video
Encode. **Revision 1 amended a signed decision record unilaterally and this
revision reverts it.**

The spike's own recommendation is narrower than revision 1 made it: *"Do not
build D3D12 Video Encode **for text quality**. If it is built later, let the
reason be AV1, vendor-neutrality, or 10-bit — not this."* One rationale is
closed. The subsystem is not.

**The headline evidence is also weaker than revision 1 claimed**, and this is
worth recording because it was quoted as settled fact:

- The "real Windows 5K capture" (`baseline/freerdp-reference/ref2-full.png`) is
  a **macOS screenshot of a FreeRDP window**, so its chroma had already been
  through a lossy codec, a client renderer and the compositor before anything
  was measured. Measuring chroma loss on chroma already destroyed upstream is
  circular for this question.
- It is tagged **DCI-P3**, while the method computed ΔE2000 "in sRGB". That
  biases ΔE *low* — in the direction that produced the headline.
- ΔE p95 0.48 sitting *below* the 0.52 rounding floor is an artefact of smooth
  content: subsampling cannot subtract error, but it can low-pass rounding noise
  out of a gradient. "Indistinguishable on a gradient" is the honest reading.
- "4:4:4 measured fractionally worse" rests on 0.68 vs 0.70 — one frame, one
  encoder, one image. That is noise cited as evidence, in a repo whose rules
  require n, min, median and max for any number reasoned from later.

**The named re-test**, which is what "deferred" obliges us to state: Intel's
hardware MFT at low-latency CBR against a real desk capture, over a long P-frame
run rather than one intra frame. The spike says explicitly that rate control
"does not transfer" and lists "anything temporal" as untested. Each is an
afternoon's work and none is an acceptance criterion yet.

## 2. The wire: one line changes

No new message type. HEVC access units ride `MSG_VIDEO_SEQ` exactly as H.264
ones do — same framing, same `u64` capture sequence, same one-AU-per-message
rule. Only the bitstream inside changes.

**`WIRE_VERSION` goes 3 → 4.** That single line is the whole compatibility
story, and revision 1 omitted it while writing three paragraphs about the
hazard it prevents.

## 3. Compatibility — `WIRE_VERSION`, and the consumer it does not cover

`WIRE_VERSION` 3 → 4, gated at `probe.rs` with a direction-aware
`VersionMismatch`, makes **both** directions fail loudly at connect: an old
client meeting an HEVC host, and a new client meeting an H.264 host. No codec
field, no serde default that is wrong in one direction.

**But it is not a protocol-wide gate, and revision 2 said it was.** There is a
third consumer of this wire. `tools/latency-spike/viewer` builds an H.264
decoder *before* connecting, treats `MSG_STATS` as opaque logging, has no
version check at all, and on a decode failure logs and continues. So a v3 spike
viewer against a v4 HEVC host **black-screens noisily rather than refusing** —
and revision 2's AC4 would have passed against the product binaries while that
stayed broken.

**The viewer gets the same gate.** It is one of the three things this tranche
must touch, not an afterthought.

`framing.rs`'s doc comment also still promises that archived captures stay
readable. No rhydra capture currently stores AU bytes — the server archive is
JSONL metadata only — so nothing is broken today, but the promise is now false
in spirit and the comment must say "H.264 or HEVC, per the wire version".

## 4. What the client shows

`session.rs:52` hardcodes `CODEC_LABEL = "AVC (rhydra)"` — the label the title
bar and HUD display. Ship without changing it and **the HUD lies about the
codec**, in a tranche whose §6 argues that a codec lying about itself is worse
than one that refuses. The label becomes a property of the negotiated stream,
not a constant.

The loop's settled answer that "transport is always visible (title/HUD/
stage-json)" points the same way.

## 5. Encoding stays exactly as it is

Revision 1 contained a table of when to tile. There is no tiling, and there is
also **no full-repaint classifier to hang one on**: `pipeline.rs:693-694` states
it plainly — *"The fast path is an overlay, not a branch: whatever happens here,
the frame still goes on to convert, encode and send as H.264 below."*

Every captured frame already produces a full-frame access unit; dirty rects are
an **additional** message carrying raw BGRA, capped at 32 rects / 96 KB. There
is no dirty-rect *encode* path anywhere in the server.

**This matters beyond the deleted table.** The decision record's "dirty rects
from DXGI drive small encodes at fractions of a millisecond", the paced spike's
"regions, not frames", and revision 1's "dirty rects remain the main path" all
describe an architecture that does not exist. The paced spike's 256×256, 640×480
and 720p cells measure a path nothing calls. Recorded here rather than left for
the next reader to rediscover; whether to *build* region encoding is a separate
question this tranche does not answer.

## 6. Host encoder

The `Encoder` trait and the async `IMFMediaEventGenerator` contract carry over.
What changes is more specific than revision 2 said, and three of the corrections
come from **our own measurements**, not from reasoning.

### 6.1 Low latency is a prerequisite, and must become a gate

The client gives every sample invalid DTS/PTS, passes zero decode flags, and
requires a picture from each AU immediately, then attributes that picture to the
current AU's capture sequence. **That is only correct because there is no
reorder delay**, which `CODECAPI_AVLowLatencyMode` is what guarantees.

Today the encoder **silently tolerates that setting being refused** — it appends
the name to a `codec_api_refused` ledger and carries on. So the invariant the
entire client depends on is assumed, not enforced.

> **Accepted low-latency mode becomes a startup gate.** If the MFT refuses it,
> the session fails at bring-up naming the setting, rather than producing a
> stream whose frames are attributed to the wrong capture sequence.

Related: revision 2 said the B-frame setting "carries over". Microsoft's HEVC
encoder does not list `CODECAPI_AVEncMPVDefaultBPictureCount` among its
properties, and the code sets it unconditionally. Its refusal must be tolerated
*explicitly* rather than by accident — and it is the low-latency gate, not this,
that delivers no-reorder. *(Whether quench's MFT actually refuses either is
unmeasured; that part is speculation and the gate makes it not matter.)*

### 6.2 Profile, tier and level — measured, not guessed

- `MF_MT_VIDEO_PROFILE` (an alias of `MF_MT_MPEG2_PROFILE`, so renaming the
  attribute alone changes nothing) must carry `eAVEncH265VProfile_Main_420_8`
  = **1**. The current defect is that it holds an **H.264** enum value.
- `MF_MT_MPEG2_LEVEL` is unset today. **Revision 2 claimed level was irrelevant
  at 1080p. That was wrong**: the server declares 60 fps, and HEVC 1920×1080×60
  requires **Level 4.1** (`eAVEncH265VLevel4_1` = 123).
- **Our own evidence already contains the answer.** The `mf-caps-probe` capture
  from quench shows the Intel MFT emitting `profile_tier_level` with
  `general_profile_idc=1`, `general_tier_flag=1`, level 123 — **Main profile,
  High tier, Level 4.1.** High tier was never a decision anyone took; it is what
  the encoder chose. §8's AC1 must state whether that is intended and then
  assert it, rather than discovering it again later.
- There is no separate tier attribute in Media Foundation. Tier is read from
  `profile_tier_level`, and `mf-caps-probe` currently discards both tier and
  level.

### 6.3 The sequence header — revision 2 guessed and guessed wrong

The attribute is **`MF_MT_MPEG_SEQUENCE_HEADER`**, not `MF_MT_MPEG2_...`, and
for H.265 Microsoft defines its contents as **Annex-B VPS/SPS/PPS with start
codes — not hvcC**. Revision 2's hvcC speculation was simply incorrect.

**And our evidence says the question is nearly moot on this hardware:** the real
Intel MFT publishes **no sequence-header blob at all**, while emitting Annex-B
VPS/SPS/PPS in band. So on quench the **in-band cache is the primary path**, not
the fallback revision 2 called it.

That makes the `annexb.rs` defect worse than "a lost fallback": it is the loss of
the only path that works here.

### 6.4 The NAL taxonomy, in full

Revision 2 named the parameter-set arithmetic and stopped. The full set:

| | H.264 | HEVC |
|---|---|---|
| NAL header | 1 byte, `b & 0x1F` | **2 bytes**, `(b[0] >> 1) & 0x3F` |
| VPS/SPS/PPS | — / 7 / 8 | **32 / 33 / 34** |
| AUD | 9 | **35** |
| IDR | 5 | **19 and 20** |
| CRA | — | **21** (all IRAP is 16–23) |

A correct parser requires two bytes and rejects `nuh_temporal_id_plus1 == 0`.

`contains_idr` is H.264-only, and **the spike viewer uses it for every keyframe
record** — so under HEVC its keyframe telemetry silently becomes false. The
server separately treats `MFSampleExtension_CleanPoint` as "IDR" and defaults a
missing attribute to false; that needs an IRAP cross-check, or an MFT that
misuses the attribute causes endless keyframe retries with parameter-set
prepending disabled.

**Revision 2 also overstated the damage in one direction, and that matters for
scoping:** the Annex-B start-code scanning itself is codec-neutral and needs no
change, and the client's four-byte NAL-length repack is correct for
VideoToolbox. "The entire file is H.264-shaped" was wrong.

### 6.5 Configuration epochs

On `MF_E_TRANSFORM_STREAM_CHANGE` the encoder accepts available type zero,
refreshes the sequence header and bumps an epoch. It does not revalidate
subtype, profile, tier, level, chroma, depth, dimensions or colour. A session can
therefore start correct, renegotiate wrong, and stay accepted.

**Every epoch revalidates as startup does, and an IRAP is required before the new
configuration paints.**

## 7. Client decoder

1. **`CMVideoFormatDescriptionCreateFromHEVCParameterSets`, which is not a
   six-argument substitution** for the H.264 call — it takes an additional
   `extensions` dictionary, and at least three parameter sets.
2. **The same NAL-header arithmetic bug exists client-side**, plus the IRAP
   taxonomy above.
3. **Hardware decode is not actually requested today.** `hardware_decoder()`
   only checks that the OS is macOS, and `VTDecompressionSessionCreate` receives
   a **null decoder specification** — so VideoToolbox may quietly use software.
   If this tranche means hardware, it must pass
   `kVTVideoDecoderSpecification_RequireHardwareAcceleratedVideoDecoder` and say
   so.
4. **Pixel format is a live hazard, from our own spike.** The callback accepts
   only full-range planar `f420`, but the VideoToolbox HEVC probe observed
   **`420v`** — video range. HEVC output in `f420` is untested, so the decoder
   must either request a format explicitly or handle both.
5. **The colour contract is undeclared end to end.** The server produces
   studio-range BT.709 NV12 while its MF types declare no nominal range, matrix,
   primaries or transfer; the client ignores buffer colour attachments and does a
   fixed full-range BT.709 conversion. Intel's defaults may happen to line up —
   that is speculation — but an undeclared contract is exactly how a washed-out
   desktop ships unnoticed. Declare it on both sides and assert it.
6. rhydra's own `VideoDecoder` trait, replacing the borrowed
   `ironrdp_egfx::decode::H264Decoder`.
7. **Not free:** `yuv420_to_rgba` runs on the CPU, on the net thread, per frame,
   and no quoted number includes it.

## 8. Acceptance criteria

Revision 2's set was audited and **every one of them could pass over a broken
HEVC path**. Rewritten so each can fail, with the false-green named under it.

- **AC1 — the negotiated stream is what we asked for, at every epoch.** Parse
  `profile_tier_level` and assert profile, **tier**, **level**, chroma, bit
  depth and dimensions — not only on the first keyframe but on **every
  configuration epoch**, with an IRAP required before the new configuration
  paints. *False-green closed: revision 2 checked one keyframe and omitted tier
  and level entirely, while our own evidence shows the encoder choosing High
  tier / Level 4.1.*
- **AC2 — the parameter-set path works against the real MFT.** On quench,
  against the Intel encoder: `nal_type` returns 32/33/34, the in-band cache
  populates, and a **bare IRAP with no cached sets is counted by a
  fail-closed counter** rather than sent bare. *False-green closed: a synthetic
  parser test passes while the live path never populates — and on this hardware
  the in-band cache is the only path, because the MFT publishes no sequence
  header.*
- **AC3 — the picture is right, and it is HEVC that made it right.** A
  text-heavy window renders correctly on quench, **with the raw-BGRA rect path
  disabled**, so nothing but decoded HEVC can paint. Tolerance stated **before**
  the run. *False-green closed: revision 2 would have passed with every HEVC
  P-frame failing, because raw rects paint text perfectly well and both paths
  report the same codec label.*
- **AC4 — all three consumers refuse a mismatch.** Old client + new host, new
  client + old host, **and the spike viewer**. *False-green closed: revision 2
  covered only `native::probe`, and the viewer has no gate at all.*
- **AC5 — latency, with zero errors.** Frame-complete p50 and p95, **n ≥ 300**
  not 30, release build, quench, stated content and bitrate, HEVC against the
  H.264 baseline at 1920×1080 — and the run is **void unless decode errors and
  drops are zero and hardware decode is confirmed**. *False-green closed:
  dropped frames and rect paints could otherwise shrink and bias the sample, and
  30 frames is not a p95 tail on a LAN with this one's documented spread.*
- **AC6 — bitrate, with its terms.** Bytes/second at a stated `bitrate_kbps`,
  **defining what is counted** (AUs only, versus AUs plus raw rects, stats,
  framing and tunnel overhead), as a distribution. *False-green closed: a stream
  that drops video looks wonderfully cheap.*
- **AC7 — changed-area coverage, replacing a tautology.** Revision 2 asked for
  "the fraction of frames that are full-frame encodes". **That answer is
  permanently 100%**, because every captured frame is encoded whatever the rect
  path did — so it could never have informed the tiling decision it existed for.
  Replaced with: changed-area coverage per frame, raw-rect hit rate, and bytes
  attributable to unchanged regions.
- **AC8 — an hour under load, with limits.** Not "sample three metrics":
  **stated ceilings** on decode errors, drops, timestamp mismatches, latency
  drift and failed parameter-set recovery, plus a pixel/chroma oracle over a
  long P-frame run — which is the case the 4:2:0 spike explicitly did not test.

## 9. What this does not do

- **No two-tile video.** Deferred with its evidence and a revisit condition
  (§1b).
- **No 4:4:4 decision.** Deferred in Arthur's words with a named re-test (§1c).
- **No AV1.** No encoder MFT is registered on quench; VideoToolbox has no
  4:4:4/4:2:2 AV1 decoder.
- **No H.264 as a second rhydra codec.** Note the saving is one-sided: the
  client keeps a full H.264 VideoToolbox decoder regardless, because the RDP
  path stays on AVC444.
- **No region encoding.** §5 records that it does not exist despite three
  documents describing it; building it is a separate question.
- **mdrdp's RDP path is untouched.**

## 10. Review record

**Revision 1: rejected** by two same-family reviewers. **Revision 2: rejected**
by a cross-family Codex review. Every finding acted on was verified against the
code or against our own recorded evidence first.

**Revision 1 → 2** — the tile design collided with the client's exactness gate
(the second tile is suppressed by design); "present when every tile has landed"
was impossible because surface 0 is the canvas; `send.rs` hoists rects ahead of
frames so one could land between two tiles; and the tile path was unreachable
anyway because the agent pins 1920×1080. Tiles deferred, which deleted the first
three findings outright.

**Revision 2 → 3** — two blockers and a set of platform-contract corrections:

- `WIRE_VERSION` is not a protocol-wide gate; the spike viewer has none (§3).
- The no-reorder invariant the client depends on is assumed, not enforced;
  low-latency mode must be a startup gate (§6.1).
- Level and tier: revision 2 said level was irrelevant at 1080p. Wrong — and
  **our own `mf-caps-probe` evidence already showed the encoder producing Main /
  High tier / Level 4.1** (§6.2).
- The sequence header is `MF_MT_MPEG_SEQUENCE_HEADER`, Annex-B not hvcC — and
  the Intel MFT publishes none, so in-band is the *primary* path here, not a
  fallback (§6.3).
- The NAL taxonomy was half-designed; and revision 2 also *overstated* the
  damage, since start-code scanning and the client's length repack are both fine
  (§6.4).
- Hardware decode is never actually requested, and the client accepts only
  `f420` while the HEVC probe observed `420v` (§7).
- **All eight acceptance criteria could pass over a broken HEVC path** (§8).

**The pattern worth keeping.** The cross-family review's own summary of what two
same-family reviewers missed: *"they followed names and architectural prose
instead of the platform contracts."* Twice now the same-family passes have found
real structural faults and missed the Windows/Apple API specifics underneath
them. That is a reason to keep both, in that order.

**Status: revision 3, ready for implementation review.** The design's shape has
been stable since revision 2; revision 3 is correctness detail, which is the
right direction for a document to converge in.
