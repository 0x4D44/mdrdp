# SPIKE — does HEVC 4:2:0 damage text and spreadsheet content at 5K?

2026-08-20. Answers open question 1 of `wrk_docs/2026.08.20 - DEC - rhydra codec choice -
HEVC 4-2-0, two tiles at 5K.md`. Measurement only; no product code was touched.

## Verdict

**No. 4:2:0 is acceptable for this product's content at 5K, and the D3D12 Video Encode
4:4:4 subsystem is not needed for image quality.** Open question 1 closes.

The reason is stronger than "it looks fine": **4:2:0 leaves luma bit-exact.** Everything a
reader uses to read — glyph shape, stroke weight, hinting, the antialiasing ramp, gridline
position — is carried in luma and survives untouched. 4:2:0 can only change the *colour* of
text, never its form. Measured two independent ways: a lossless 4:2:0 encode round-trips
PSNR `inf` on Y, and a no-codec 4:4:4→4:2:0→4:4:4 pass gives Y PSNR `inf` with SSIM
`1.000000`.

On a **real** Windows 5K desktop capture, 4:2:0's entire chroma cost lands *below* the
RGB↔YUV conversion floor — it is not merely acceptable, it is unmeasurable against the
noise the pipeline already has.

One genuine degradation exists and is worth knowing about: **1-device-pixel saturated line
art stripes** (§5). It falls almost entirely outside the 2× HiDPI configuration the
decision assumes, and no bitrate fixes it, so it is a thing to note rather than act on.

## 1. Method

| | |
|---|---|
| Encoder | `libx265` 4.2+1-e444744 via ffmpeg 8.1.2, `-preset medium`, `keyint=1`, `bframes=0` |
| Resolution | 5120×2880, one frame in, one frame out (a full 5K IDR repaint) |
| Colour | full-range BT.709 throughout; RGB→YUV444 once, then everything downstream |
| Chroma formats | `-pix_fmt yuv420p` and `-pix_fmt yuv444p` from the identical YUV444 source |
| Metrics | per-plane PSNR/SSIM (ffmpeg `psnr`/`ssim`), plus CIEDE2000 ΔE per region in sRGB |

Everything is in
`wrk_docs/evidence/2026.08.20 - hevc-420-text-spike/measurements.txt`, with the generator
and analysis scripts beside it.

### The gate that made the numbers trustworthy

Every run had to satisfy: **a lossless 4:4:4 encode must round-trip bit-exact (PSNR `inf`
on all three planes), and a lossless 4:2:0 encode must be `inf` on Y.** That is not
ceremony. The first version of this pipeline failed it — swscale was silently inserting a
tv→pc range expansion ahead of the encoder, which pinned every luma PSNR at ~28 dB
regardless of bitrate. Without the gate I would have published a table where qp16 and
5 Mbps looked identical and concluded something confident and wrong. Fixed by tagging the
raw input `setparams=range=pc`.

A second control: the no-codec pass (swscale 4:4:4→4:2:0→4:4:4) agrees with the lossless
4:2:0 encode to six significant figures — `u 35.827100 / v 33.610488` both ways. x265's
chroma downsampler and swscale's are the same filter, so "4:2:0 subsampling loss" is one
number, not an encoder detail.

### Test content

**Real (priority): `baseline/freerdp-reference/ref2-full.png`** — a genuine 5120×2880
Windows capture already in the repo, at exactly the target resolution, with real
ClearType-rendered UI text. It is the only real 5K Windows frame the repo holds. Its
weakness: it is the *sign-in screen* — a smooth blue gradient plus one line of body text
and two buttons. It is not a text-heavy desk, so on its own it would flatter 4:2:0.

**Synthetic (to cover what the real capture doesn't): a 5120×2880 four-quadrant desk**
(`evidence/.../src-testcard-5120x2880.png`, generator `make_testcard.py`) — a dark-theme
syntax-highlighted code editor and an Excel-like sheet at 2× HiDPI sizes, the same two at
1× (100% scaling), and a chroma-stress quadrant: simulated ClearType, saturated text on
black and on white, 1/2/3-pixel coloured lines, an ANSI terminal, and an equiluminant
chroma-resolution target. Fonts are the Windows-metric set actually present on this Mac
(Tahoma, Arial, Courier New, Menlo).

**The ClearType is simulated, not captured.** Glyph coverage is sampled at 3× horizontal
resolution and run through the classic 5-tap `[1,2,3,2,1]/9` subpixel FIR, and the triplets
become the R,G,B coverages of one output pixel — which is what Windows does. It omits
ClearType's gamma correction, and my vertical antialiasing is better than classic
ClearType's. So it is the right mechanism, not a byte-exact reproduction.

The synthetic card is deliberately adversarial. Reading the two sources together is the
point: the true answer sits between them, and the gap between them (§3) is the honest
measure of how far I stacked the deck.

### Exact commands

```bash
# reference: RGB -> YUV444, full-range BT.709, once
ffmpeg -i src.png -vf "scale=out_color_matrix=bt709:out_range=full" \
       -pix_fmt yuv444p -f rawvideo ref444.yuv

# encode (PF = yuv420p | yuv444p); psy-rd=0 -- see the x265 trap below
SETP="setparams=range=pc:colorspace=bt709:color_primaries=bt709:color_trc=bt709"
ffmpeg -r 30 -f rawvideo -pix_fmt yuv444p -s 5120x2880 -i ref444.yuv -vf "$SETP" \
       -c:v libx265 -preset medium -pix_fmt $PF -b:v 20M \
       -x265-params "keyint=1:bframes=0:range=full:psy-rd=0" \
       -color_range pc -colorspace bt709 -f hevc enc.265

# decode back to a common YUV444 space, and to RGB for the crops
ffmpeg -i enc.265 -vf "$SETP" -pix_fmt yuv444p -f rawvideo dec.yuv
ffmpeg -i enc.265 -vf "$SETP,scale=out_color_matrix=bt709:in_range=full:out_range=full" \
       -pix_fmt rgb24 -frames:v 1 dec.png

# metrics
ffmpeg -f rawvideo -pix_fmt yuv444p -s 5120x2880 -i ref444.yuv \
       -f rawvideo -pix_fmt yuv444p -s 5120x2880 -i dec.yuv -lavfi psnr -f null -
```

**An x265 trap worth recording.** With `psy-rd` on (the default), x265 prints *"halving the
quality when psy-rd is enabled for 444 input. Setting cbQpOffset = 6 and crQpOffset = 6"*
and **ignores an explicit `cbQpOffset=0`** — verified by byte-identical output with and
without the override. Only `psy-rd=0` clears it. Every 4:4:4 figure in §3 of the
measurement log carries that +6 chroma-QP handicap; the headline tables here use
`psy-rd=0` on both formats so the two are actually comparable. It biases *against* 4:4:4,
so leaving it in would have made 4:2:0 look better than it is.

## 2. Luma is untouched — the load-bearing result

| | PSNR Y | SSIM Y | PSNR U | PSNR V |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| no codec: 4:4:4 → 4:2:0 → 4:4:4 | **inf** | **1.000000** | 35.83 | 33.61 |
| lossless HEVC 4:2:0 | **inf** | **1.000000** | 35.83 | 33.61 |
| lossless HEVC 4:4:4 | inf | 1.000000 | inf | inf |

4:2:0 discards three quarters of the chroma samples and **not one luma sample**. This is
why the question "does 4:2:0 damage text" has a structural answer rather than a taste-based
one: text shape is a luma signal. What is at risk is only the colour of small coloured
things.

## 3. Real content vs the adversarial card

Pure subsampling cost (lossless encode, so the *only* loss is 4:2:0 itself):

| source | PSNR U | PSNR V | whole-frame ΔE2000 p95 |
|---|---|---|---|
| **real Windows 5K capture** | **54.23** | **58.59** | **0.48** |
| synthetic desk card | 35.83 | 33.61 | 1.73 |

The real frame has **18–25 dB less chroma detail to lose.** For scale: the *lossless 4:4:4*
path on the same real frame measures ΔE p95 **0.52** — that is the irreducible RGB↔YUV
rounding floor. 4:2:0's subsampling loss (0.48) is **below** it. On real Windows desktop
content, 4:2:0 costs less than the colour-space conversion the pipeline performs anyway.

At a realistic IDR (~40 KB for this frame), whole-frame ΔE p95 is **0.68 for 4:2:0** against
**0.70 for 4:4:4** — 4:2:0 is fractionally *better*, because the bits it does not spend on
chroma go to luma instead. And 4:4:4 cost 39% more bytes to get there (55,314 vs 39,901).

**Caveat, stated plainly:** the real capture is a sign-in screen, not a spreadsheet. It
under-represents a working desk. The synthetic card over-represents one. The truth is
between, and §4 says which way.

## 4. Where 4:2:0 lands on desk content

Synthetic card, CIEDE2000 per region. `4:2:0 subsampling only` is the lossless encode — the
floor no bitrate can beat. ΔE 2 is "noticeable side by side"; ΔE 5 is "obvious".

| region | 4:2:0 subsampling only, ΔE p95 | % pixels ΔE>2 | % ΔE>5 |
|---|---|---|---|
| code editor, dark, 2× HiDPI | 0.51 | 3.2 | 1.4 |
| spreadsheet, light, 2× HiDPI | 0.74 | 1.9 | 0.7 |
| code editor, dark, 1× | 0.00 | 2.5 | 1.5 |
| spreadsheet, light, 1× (1px gridlines) | 0.00 | 1.3 | 0.4 |
| coloured body text, 1× (12px) | 2.73 | 5.7 | 3.3 |
| saturated text on black | 2.97 | 5.9 | 3.5 |
| saturated text on white | 3.71 | 7.2 | 3.8 |
| ANSI terminal on black | 5.40 | 9.3 | 5.4 |
| ClearType, 100% scaling | 10.37 | 11.5 | 8.6 |
| ClearType, 200% HiDPI | 8.01 | 13.1 | 8.4 |
| **thin coloured lines, 1/2/3px** | **17.52** | **33.5** | **22.8** |
| **equiluminant chroma target** | **40.48** | **42.3** | **22.2** |

The four workhorse regions — code and spreadsheet, dark and light, at both scalings — sit at
ΔE p95 **0.00–0.74**, i.e. below the just-noticeable threshold for 95% of pixels. The
1× spreadsheet with 1-pixel gridlines, which I expected to be a problem, is **0.00**:
gridlines are grey, so they are a pure luma signal and 4:2:0 does not see them.

Crops confirm it visually. At 4× magnification, `crops/05-spreadsheet-1x-grid.png` and
`crops/12-coloured-body-text-1x.png` show source, 4:4:4 and 4:2:0 as indistinguishable.
`crops/13-REAL-windows-cleartype.png` — real Windows white-on-blue body text at 5×
magnification — likewise.

On the two visibly-affected regions, note that at a realistic IDR the *codec quantisation*
is the larger term almost everywhere: e.g. saturated text on black is ΔE p95 2.97 from
subsampling but 5.38 after a 284 KB encode. Only the thin-lines and equiluminant regions are
subsampling-dominated.

*(One confound to be honest about: at the same `-b:v`, 4:4:4 took 18% more bytes on this
card, so the "4:2:0 at 20M vs 4:4:4 at 20M" columns in the raw log are not a matched-byte
comparison and 4:4:4's advantage there is partly just more bits. The lossless column, which
is the one this section quotes, has no such confound.)*

## 5. What actually degrades

### 5a. One-device-pixel saturated line art — real, and the only one that matters

`crops/09-thin-coloured-lines.png` and its `-1to1` companion. A run of 1-device-pixel
coloured vertical lines comes back **striped**: alternate lines render dark and muddy
instead of uniformly bright. Visible at 1:1, not just magnified. ΔE p95 17.5, a fifth of the
region above ΔE 5.

Cause: two adjacent luma columns share one chroma sample, so a 1px line lands either on a
chroma sample position or between two, and its colour is either kept or averaged with the
background. At **2 device px** the artefact largely goes; at **3 px** it is gone.

Scope: at 200% HiDPI a "1-pixel" UI line is 2 device px, so this mostly falls outside the
configuration the decision assumes. At 100% scaling — 5120×2880 with no scaling — coloured
chart series, focus rings, 1px coloured borders and underlines will show it. No mitigation
exists below 4:4:4; adding bitrate does nothing (§6).

### 5b. Pure-chroma detail at 1px vanishes entirely

`crops/10-equiluminant-target.png`. Alternating 1px columns of pure red and a blue-green
chosen for identical BT.709 luma collapse to **flat magenta** under 4:2:0 — the pattern
does not degrade, it disappears. ΔE p95 40.5. At 2px columns it survives; at 4px it is
clean.

This is a real property of 4:2:0 and worth seeing once, but it is a test pattern. Desktop
UI essentially never encodes information in chroma alone at 1px with matched luma — if it
did, the content would be invisible to colour-blind users too. **Do not weight this
against the decision.**

### 5c. Not a degradation: chroma bleed around saturated glyphs

At 3× magnification, saturated text on black shows a coloured halo spilling 1–2 px around
each glyph (`crops/07-saturated-on-black.png`). At **1:1** — the size a user sees — the same
crop is indistinguishable from 4:4:4 (`...-1to1.png`). Magnification manufactured that
finding; I include both views so the artefact cannot be quoted without its scale.

## 6. Bitrate cannot rescue 4:2:0 chroma — it plateaus immediately

Each row is one rate-control setting; the two formats produce different byte counts at the
same setting, so both are shown.

| setting | 4:2:0 bytes | 4:2:0 PSNR U / V | 4:4:4 bytes | 4:4:4 PSNR U / V |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `-b:v 5M` | 244,002 | 34.97 / 33.07 | 237,034 | 39.36 / 39.23 |
| `-b:v 20M` | 286,227 | 35.16 / 33.20 | 289,206 | 41.23 / 41.19 |
| `-b:v 50M` | 450,749 | 35.61 / 33.49 | 483,454 | 47.42 / 47.56 |
| `qp=16` | 804,728 | 35.81 / 33.60 | 920,214 | 58.72 / 58.75 |
| lossless | 1,025,706 | 35.83 / 33.61 | 1,282,659 | inf |

*(4:4:4 rows here carry the +6 chroma-QP handicap described in §1, so the real 4:4:4 curve
is better still.)*

From 244 KB to lossless — **4.2× the bits** — 4:2:0 chroma moves **0.85 dB on U and
0.54 dB on V**. The subsampling *is* the floor, and it is already reached at the lowest
rate tested. Luma over the same range climbs 40.5 dB → inf.

Two consequences worth carrying into rhydra:

1. **Spending bits on a 4:2:0 stream to improve colour is wasted.** Rate-control tuning can
   only buy luma. That is a useful constraint on any future quality knob.
2. **If 4:2:0 ever proves inadequate, only 4:4:4 fixes it.** There is no cheaper lever. The
   D3D12 work stays the correct answer to a question we have now established we are not
   asking.

## 7. The doc's ClearType hypothesis: mechanism right, scaling backwards

The decision record predicts: *"At 2× HiDPI, chroma still lands at ~110 ppi, and what 4:2:0
mainly destroys is ClearType subpixel colour — arguably an improvement."*

Measured fringe chroma energy — mean `|Cb−128| + |Cr−128|` over the text block, which is
exactly the subpixel colour ClearType adds and grayscale antialiasing does not:

| | source | after 4:2:0 | removed |
|---|---|---|---|
| 100% scaling, 13px device text | 7.85 | 3.17 | **60%** |
| 200% HiDPI, 26px device text | 7.20 | 6.19 | **14%** |
| grayscale-AA control | 0.00 | 0.00 | — |

**4:2:0 does neutralise ClearType fringes — at 100% scaling, which is not the case the doc
was arguing about.** At the 2× HiDPI it assumes, glyph stems are 2 device px and the
fringes largely survive the 2×2 chroma block: only 14% of the fringe colour is removed, and
`crops/02-cleartype-200pct.png` shows source and 4:2:0 as near-identical.

So the hypothesis has the mechanism right and the scaling dependence inverted. Its
*conclusion* survives intact, for a slightly different reason than stated: at 100% the
fringes are cleaned up (an improvement, as argued), and at 200% they are preserved
(no change). Neither outcome touches legibility, because neither touches luma.
`crops/01-cleartype-100pct.png` shows the 100% case — glyph shapes identical across all
four rows, colour visibly more neutral under 4:2:0.

## 8. What this does and does not prove

**Transfers to rhydra:**
- The subsampling loss itself. It is arithmetic performed on the source before any codec
  sees it, and §1's control shows x265's downsampler and swscale's produce identical
  results. Intel's MFT will discard the same samples.
- That luma is untouched by chroma format. Structural to 4:2:0, not an encoder choice.
- The chroma plateau (§6) — a consequence of the subsampling floor, not of x265's rate
  control.

**Does not transfer:**
- **Rate control.** libx265 ABR is not Intel's MFT CBR. The absolute quality at a given
  byte count, and the H.264 CBR anomaly in the decision record's open question 3, say
  nothing to each other.
- Deblocking/SAO strength and intra-prediction quality. A hardware encoder at 8 ms will be
  worse than `-preset medium` at both, equally for both chroma formats.
- **Chroma upsampling.** I used swscale's default (bicubic). rhydra's renderer will do it
  on the GPU, probably bilinear or in the YUV→RGB sampler. Expect the thin-line artefact in
  §5a to look slightly different — likely worse with nearest-neighbour.

**Not tested at all:**
- **Anything temporal.** This is one intra frame. rhydra's real path is mostly dirty-rect
  P-frames, and nothing here speaks to chroma drift over a long P-frame run, or to chroma
  lag if rhydra ever adopts an AVC444-style luma-only update.
- **A real text-heavy Windows 5K frame.** The one real capture available is a sign-in
  screen. The strongest cheap follow-up is to repeat §4 and §5 against a real
  spreadsheet-and-terminal 5K capture once one exists — it would replace the synthetic card
  with the thing it approximates.
- **A human viewing-distance study.** I judged the 1:1 crops myself, on this 5120×2880
  display, at normal seating distance.

## 9. Recommendation

1. **Keep HEVC 4:2:0.** Close open question 1 of the decision record as answered: image
   quality does not require 4:4:4.
2. **Do not build D3D12 Video Encode for text quality.** If it is built later, let the
   reason be AV1, vendor-neutrality, or 10-bit — not this.
3. **Record the 1-device-pixel line artefact** (§5a) as a known, unfixable-below-4:4:4
   property, scoped to 100% scaling. Note it; do not act on it.
4. **Do not spend bits chasing colour** on a 4:2:0 stream (§6) — any future quality knob
   should be understood as a luma knob.
