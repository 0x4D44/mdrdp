# PARK — tranche 6 (audio) needs a render endpoint on the host

**Status: parked, awaiting Arthur.** Not blocked on code, design or effort. Blocked
on a fact about quench that no amount of client work gets around.

## The finding

quench has **no active audio render endpoint**. WASAPI's own answer, from
`tools/audio-probe` run on the box:

```
audio-probe: 6 render endpoint(s)
  endpoint 0: not-present (0x00000004)
  endpoint 1: not-present (0x00000004)
  endpoint 2: not-present (0x00000004)
  endpoint 3: unplugged (0x00000008)
  endpoint 4: unplugged (0x00000008)
  endpoint 5: not-present (0x00000004)
audio-probe: 0 ACTIVE
audio-probe: default render endpoint: NONE (Element not found. (0x80070490))
```

The three unplugged/not-present Realtek and display-audio endpoints are what a
headless box looks like: the sound hardware is present and `Audiosrv` is running,
but nothing is plugged into the jack and no monitor is carrying HDMI audio, so
Windows keeps every endpoint inactive.

**This was worth measuring rather than assuming.** `Win32_SoundDevice` reports
"Realtek High Definition Audio — Status OK", and both audio services are Running.
By every check short of asking WASAPI, the host looks ready for audio.

## Why it stops the tranche, and why it is not merely a capture problem

RDPSND-equivalent audio works by loopback capture: attach an `IAudioClient` to the
default **render** endpoint with `AUDCLNT_STREAMFLAGS_LOOPBACK` and read what the
session is playing. No default render endpoint, nothing to attach to.

The deeper point is the one that matters for the decision: **with no render
endpoint, applications on quench cannot play audio in the first place.** They fail
to open their own audio clients. So this is not "our capture path needs a
workaround" — there is genuinely no audio on that host to send. Any client-side
work would be built against, and validated by, nothing.

Per-process loopback (`VIRTUAL_AUDIO_DEVICE_PROCESS_LOOPBACK`, Windows 10 20H1+)
does not rescue it, for the same reason: it captures what a process renders, and
with no endpoint nothing renders.

## The question for Arthur

**How should a rhydra host acquire a render endpoint?** Four viable answers, cheapest
first:

1. **A physical jack plug.** A 3.5 mm dummy plug in quench's audio socket makes the
   Realtek endpoint ACTIVE immediately. No software, no config, instantly
   reversible — but it needs someone at the machine, and it does not generalise to
   other hosts.
2. **Realtek jack-detection override.** Disabling jack detection makes an unplugged
   endpoint report ACTIVE. No new software, but it is driver-specific, likely needs
   a reboot (a hard stop for this loop without your word), and may not survive a
   driver update.
3. **A virtual audio driver** (VB-Cable, Scream, VAC). This is how the rest of the
   industry solves headless audio, and it generalises to every host. It is
   third-party kernel-mode software on a fleet box, which is a dependency decision
   and yours to take.
4. **Descope for endpoint-less hosts.** Ship audio for hosts that have an endpoint,
   and have the health ladder and `--doctor` report "no audio endpoint — audio
   unavailable" as a rung. Defensible on the merits: a headless server with no sound
   card has no audio to redirect. The cost is that quench is our only test box, so
   we could not validate the feature at all.

**My recommendation: 1 for now, 3 if audio is meant to work on any fleet host, and
4 regardless.** A dummy plug unblocks the tranche this week at zero risk. Option 4
is not an alternative to the others — the ladder should report a missing endpoint
whatever we choose, because the alternative is audio that is silently absent, which
is the exact failure mode requirement 4 (no visibility) exists to prevent.

Note that (1) and (2) both make the endpoint active *on the console session*, which
is the session rhydra owns — so either genuinely unblocks the design as written.

## What was not built, and deliberately so

The portable half of tranche 6 is buildable today without quench: the aux-channel
priority sender (audio FIFO ranking above the clipboard's keep-latest slot), the
`MSG_AUDIO` wire message, and the Mac-side path — which is nearly free, because
`src/audio.rs` already has `AudioRing`, `AudioPlayback`, PCM16 decode, channel
reconciliation and resampling from the RDP path.

I have not built it. Half a feature that cannot be validated end-to-end is the
checkpoint anti-pattern, and worse, the capture side's real behaviour — mix format,
buffer period, how silence is reported — is what should inform the wire format and
the jitter policy. Designing those against an assumption, with no way to test the
assumption, is how you get a wire format you have to break later.

## Design notes banked for when it unparks

- **Codec: raw PCM16 LE, not Opus.** 48 kHz stereo 16-bit is ~1.5 Mbps, against
  video's tens of Mbps on this LAN — a ~5% tax, and it adds no dependency (an Opus
  crate would need your word). Revisit only if rhydra ever runs over a WAN.
- **The priority separation is weaker than it looks, and should be stated plainly.**
  Video (9500) and aux (9503) are separate TCP connections, but they share one ssh
  tunnel, so ssh interleaves them and aux traffic *can* delay video at the transport
  layer. Strict priority within the aux channel does not change that. Keeping audio
  to a bounded ~1.5 Mbps is the actual mitigation, and it should be measured, not
  assumed.
- **`Slot` is wrong for audio and must not simply be reused.** It is depth-1
  keep-latest, which is correct for a clipboard (a superseded payload was never the
  current content) and destroys a continuous stream. Audio needs a bounded FIFO that
  drops oldest when behind. The clean shape is one outbox holding both, draining
  audio first.
- **Clock drift is the soak risk.** Host capture clock and Mac playback clock differ
  by ppm and accumulate over hours — the audio analogue of requirement 3 (latency
  degrades over a long session). `AudioRing` already writes silence on underrun; the
  missing half is a policy for persistent over-fill.

## Evidence

`tools/audio-probe` is committed rather than thrown away: it is the check that
answers this question on any host, and it is the check `--doctor` should eventually
make. It is read-only — it initialises a loopback client and drops it without
`Start()`, so it cannot disturb anything already playing. Deployed at
`C:/mdrdp/audio-probe.exe` on quench for re-running after any of options 1-3.
