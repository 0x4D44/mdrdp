# Adaptive AVC444 presentation wait

<!-- STATUS: Implemented; visual verification pending -->

Arthur reports improved but persistent high-frequency chroma flicker in 0.1.248.
Replace the fixed first-luma wait with bounded, per-session learning. Preserve
ordered decoding, exact accepted coverage, frame boundaries and snapshot-token
acknowledgements. This supersedes the fixed-duration part of the earlier wait
design, not its presentation state machine.

## Timing evidence is separate from presentation state

A presentation timeout destroys its pending coverage ledger. Learning from that
ledger alone would censor every late arrival and never learn to wait longer.
Keep small timing probes independently; timeout and successful acknowledgement
must not erase them.

Each probe holds its original footprint, remaining chroma coverage and explicit
start time. On each mapped callback:

1. Discard probes older than 500 ms, without manufacturing a timing sample.
2. New luma overlapping an original footprint invalidates that whole probe.
   Do not open a replacement probe for that callback: a later chroma update
   might belong to the superseded luma and create an artificially short gap.
3. Subtract accepted chroma coverage from surviving probes. Full coverage
   records one elapsed-time sample; partial coverage stays open.
4. Open one probe only for nonempty luma with empty chroma and no collision.
   Combined luma/chroma updates never open a probe, but can complete older,
   spatially separate probes.

An empty chroma list is not proof of wire LC1: a skipped LC0 chroma pass can
also produce it. These are spatial completion observations, not protocol pair
identities or network RTT measurements. Original footprints matter even after
partial chroma: later luma in an already-completed portion invalidates the cohort.

Limit probes to eight, all stored region pieces to 256, and callback input to
512 regions. Drop timing metadata on overflow, never decoder input. Surface,
mapping, graphics-reset and reveal changes invalidate probes. Visible non-AVC
writes do too, including after a presentation timeout. Preserve learned session
history across those changes.

## Delay policy

Start at 50 ms. Keep the last 32 valid completed gaps. The target is their
maximum plus 8 ms, clamped to 16–150 ms. Raise immediately; lower by at most
1 ms after four valid samples with a lower target. Reset downward credit when
the target is at least the current delay. This asymmetric response protects
recent slow arrivals without oscillating after every fast one.

Missing, expired, superseded and budget-dropped probes are not successful
samples. They do not drive the estimate in either direction. With no usable
new evidence, retain the estimate rather than claim the server became faster.

Read the selected delay once when arming a new pending batch. Learning and
later luma cannot extend an armed deadline. Accepted complete chroma coverage
still releases presentation immediately. For callbacks containing both luma
and chroma, cap the selected wait at the previous 50 ms to avoid increasing
the existing combined-update residual wait. Do not alter that coverage policy
based on an unverified assumption that residual chroma can never arrive.

A Fable consultation checked the independent tracker and bounded policy. The
lead adopted overlap refusal, combined-update exclusion and retained history.
The lead chose a recent maximum instead of p90: intentional tail misses conflict
with reducing flicker. The suggestion to lower on censored probes was rejected:
absence of chroma does not establish a shorter completion time. The suggested
"chroma after copy" counter is not an on-glass flicker count and is not added.

## Evidence and limits

Trace only timing metadata: valid gaps, learned and selected waits, probe counts
and drop reasons. Existing default diagnostics retain their two 8 MiB rotating
files. Do not log pixels, terminal contents, clipboard data or credentials.

Deterministic clock tests cover late completion after timeout and ack, increase
and slow decrease, bounds, partial coverage, overlap ambiguity, combined-update
exclusion, lifecycle and non-AVC invalidation, budget fallback, and immutable
armed deadlines. Make new assertions fail under targeted mutations before
accepting green. Run focused surface, window and graphics tests plus formatting
and Clippy on the final integration candidate.

This is not a guarantee that luma and chroma always appear together. Delays
above 150 ms still time out. Repeated overlapping updates can prevent reliable
sampling. Unrelated covering chroma can look like a late completion. Ready-token
snapshot supersession can include newer luma early; compositor scheduling can
delay a draw beyond the policy deadline. A live affected release session is
still required to judge the visual result. No live client is launched or replaced
as part of this implementation.
